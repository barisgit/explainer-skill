# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Mermaid diagrams are drawn by pele, the renderer T3 Code uses, instead of beautiful-mermaid with ELK. Many edges into one node no longer stack their arrowheads, and pack draws a diagram about 20x faster. pack now reads Mermaid 12's syntax, so notes, titles, `@{ shape: ... }`, `--x`/`--o` edge ends and markdown strings (`"`**bold** label`"`) draw instead of failing. Node text is 15 px and edge labels 13 px; on wide screens they grow up to 21 and 18.2 px. A pair's second unnamed edge keeps the id `L_a_b_1`, and a nested state's start is now `<state>_start` (was `_start2`). `classDef` and `style` take plain colours only: use `:::info`, `:::ok`, `:::warn` or `:::bad` for the kit's colours.
- Mermaid and schematic drawings can grow up to 40% beyond their base size on wide screens, with typical labels reaching 18.2 px. Phone and print sizing stays the same.
- Use `:::info`, `:::ok`, `:::warn` or `:::bad` on a Mermaid flowchart node, or assign the role with `class a,b info`. It tints the node and colours its outline and label from the kit tokens in both themes. Explicit `classDef` and `style` statements still work; `pack` warns when they use fixed paint.
- The default look is Quiet+: the same warm paper with larger titles and section headings, less space above the title, an Explore readout set as the figure's key number (larger, semibold, tabular), and a slightly deeper `--info`. The guide asks for one large figure carrying the answer on the first screen and a system map's parts coloured by role. Pages that set `--size-headline`, `--size-display` or `--page-top` keep their values.
- A Stepper's Mermaid or schematic diagram is at least as wide as the text column on screens from 40rem, short of doubling its labels or passing 60% of the window's height. The guide asks for a stacked layout rather than one long row of nodes, which reads as a thin strip.

## v0.20.0

- A `Stepper` shows the scrubber by default from five steps. Shorter steppers still show Back, Next and the count. Set `scrubber` to show it sooner or `scrubber={false}` to hide it; Play is still opt-in (revises #190).
- A Stepper eases changes in its diagram parts and linked terms and fades in the new caption when the reader steps. Opening, restoring saved controls, reduced motion and print stay still. Explore's ref-state changes no longer fade; Plot still tweens between frames (#337).
- Readers can pick System, Light or Dark at the top of any page, including offline. The browser remembers their choice and applies it to open tabs that share local storage. Frontmatter `scheme: light` or `scheme: dark` sets the initial choice, not a lock (#336).
- A bare Mermaid or schematic fence in a full-width `.beside` now aligns its list to the prose column, like a drawing in a figure. Spare width stays after the diagram; phones still stack (#319).
- `check` still loads, scrolls and probes the page and its views but no longer saves PNGs by default. Add `--shots` for the previous first-screen and full-page PNGs; `--do shot:<css>` still saves just the requested elements without `--shots`. `--out` and `--tag` are ignored with a notice when no captures are requested. JSON omits `out` and PNG paths when not applicable; `shots` and `views.captured` still describe what was checked (#335).
- The masthead is the title alone. Paragraphs under the `#` (and under a View's `#`) are ordinary body text: they no longer become a `.lede`, a grey `.meta` line or a `.next` action, and the kit no longer styles those classes in the masthead. An `em` in a heading still marks its phrase in the accent. Name what the page or section covers in its title or heading, and put the finding in the first sentence or figure; the guide and example now do so, and step 4 ends with a pass that cuts what loses nothing (#329).
- Asked to explain a whole system, the skill now builds a visual tour rather than a census: one map of the whole first, each area and each part (a section or view) opening with its own diagram or chart, examples drawn as walkthroughs (a sequence diagram or Stepper following one request), and any register of every file or module kept as reference behind it. SKILL.md asks for a figure plan before writing, naming the diagram or chart that carries each section and each view, and a second example, `example/tour/page.mdx`, shows a short tour with its parts in views (#331).

## v0.18.0

- Pages read closer to a documentation site. A hairline sits under the masthead. Sections, including chapters in Views, are separated by space alone: about 3rem instead of a gap that grew with the window's height to 8rem. Write `---` between blank lines where you want a quiet divider; `.minor` sections sit closer. Prose lines are a little further apart (1.65), an `h3` is a step larger than the text with room above it, a table's row names are no longer bold, and a table in the text's column takes the width its columns need instead of the column's. A page rule that relied on `--section-gap`'s old size may need a look.
- A `<details>` fold in the text keeps the text's measure, and its summary is one line of text that wraps under itself: a formula in it (`<summary>Why the factor $2$?</summary>`) no longer splits the question into columns on a phone.
- A paragraph of one `$$...$$` on a single line is a display equation, centred and scrolling like one written with `$$` on lines of their own; it used to be a small inline formula at the line's start.
- On a phone a Markdown table or `Table` of two columns (a formula sheet: a name, then its formula) stacks each row, its name over its value, under its stacked column heads, instead of cutting the formula at the screen's edge; pack and `Table` mark it `data-stack`, which a `table.compare` written by hand opts in with. Wider tables still scroll with their row names in view, which now keep their row's hairline.
- A plain `<figure>` with an image and `<figcaption>` sits on the column's start with its caption as a label, as a `.fig` does.
- Set a task the reader solves in `<aside class="callout">` with a `<b>` naming it first, and steps done in order as a numbered list inside `<div class="procedure">`, which rings each number on a hairline (`kit theme`).
- Numeric Questions accept the minus sign `−` as well as `-`. If the accepted range includes negative numbers, the field uses the text keyboard so iPhone readers can type a minus.
- The guide keeps answer headings for decision pages and reports and asks for short label headings ("Mixing") on study, how-to and reference pages, the answer in the first sentence or figure. Every page shows each section's main path and puts supporting derivations, further examples, edge cases and textbook references in `<details>` folds labelled with the question they answer. Prose is plain: one idea per paragraph, bold only where a term is defined, citations and equation numbers at a paragraph's end.
- Write a reminder the reader may need once, as `<Recall id="spectrum">One short paragraph.</Recall>`, and mark each mention with `<Term recall="spectrum">spectrum</Term>`: tapping the term opens the reminder right under it and tapping again closes it; print shows it at the first mention. `pack` fails on a `Term` naming no `Recall` and warns about a `Recall` nobody mentions.
- A prerequisite too long for a reminder goes in a `View`, and `<Term view="spectrum">spectrum</Term>` leads to it: a link in the sentence's ink with a link's underline, where a reminder's term has a dotted one. `pack` fails on a `Term` leading to no `View`. Back, and a view's Back link, now return the reader to the link they left from, at its place in the window and focused, on the overview and in a view (Forward too); focus used to be lost.
- A lit term now has a thin accent outline and solid underline, so it is as easy to find as its linked diagram part, even in dark mode. Inline stepper highlights use the same treatment without shifting the text.
- A reply note is written and edited in a small popover under the text or part it is about, which stays highlighted while you write. A saved note stays marked: click its highlight, or its margin button from the keyboard, to edit or delete it in place. Marks stay with the reader across reloads and do not print. Pages set the new popover buttons' texts with the `deleteNote` and `done` labels.

## v0.17.0

- `check --viewport 1920x1080` adds a size to the default desktop and narrow checks, with its dimensions in the report and PNG names. Repeat it to check several sizes.
- `check --budget 60` gives each scroll sweep and the one kit-control sweep more time and lifts the default 12-control cap on long pages.
- `check --do click:<css>` now clicks SVG parts, so a step can pin a linked Mermaid or schematic part before capturing it. A coordinate click that opens a new window reports and closes it instead of timing out.

- Put a list and its drawing in `<div class="beside" data-width="full">` to reach both sides of the prose column without crowding the contents rail. The pinned diagram fits the window height where its labels stay readable, leaving spare width for the list. Phones still stack.

- Schematic fences draw `0`/`gnd`/`GND` as a triangle and reserve `earth` (three bars) and `chassis` (hatched bar) as separate, linkable grounds. Rename a former `earth` or `chassis` net to keep it routed. Use `tvs`, `tvs2`, `fuse`, `ptc` or `varistor` for input protection. If a first net uses one of the new keywords, write the part kind explicitly.

- `pack` keeps adjacent closing punctuation with an inline formula so it cannot start the next line alone.
- A `Question` with a numeric `answer`, optional `tolerance` and `unit` checks calculations on the page and then shows the worked solution. Readers use the page's decimal separator; the answer is kept locally and stays out of replies.

- Pointing at a linked part is quieter (#245): a lit diagram or schematic part is outlined in the accent while its text and its wires keep their ink, and the rest of the figure fades to 75 % instead of 30 %, the same whether you hover the part or the term that names it. A click on a term no longer leaves it lit through its focus after the pointer leaves; keyboard focus still lights it, and a stepper's parts not reached yet stay faded while another part is lit.
- Packed Mermaid flowcharts keep routed bends when a shared edge trunk would cut through a node, including the phone layout.
- A long page can group its contents rail: each `##` is a group and its `###` topics are listed indented under it; the current topic and its group follow the scroll, and the rail scrolls to keep them in sight. A page without `###` keeps its rail as it was.
- A page can be split into `.mdx` parts: `import Intro from "./parts/intro.mdx"` and `<Intro />` on a line of its own (at the page's root or in a `View`) is laid out as if written there, so its `#` and `##` give the masthead, sections and rail. A part's imports join the page's (paths kept relative to the part), and `pack` fails on a name two files bind to different things. A part given props, a layout or an `import()` of its own stays a component, and `pack` warns when its headings then make no sections.
- `pack` fails on a JSX tag that starts with a small letter and names an import (`<s10_Mean />`), which MDX writes as an HTML element that draws nothing: import the component under a capitalised name.
- Each section's answer is a figure, table or diagram by default, with prose interpreting it; qualitative subjects have forms too (a divergence as a flowchart, what each side has as a matrix with a status column, a history as a timeline). Link a packed page rather than embed it in a host's inline frame.
- `check` reports the page's reading load on arrival (words on the first screen and on load, number of sections, bare words per section) and warns about a section with more than 175 bare words and no figure, table, diagram or code: make its answer a figure and keep the prose to interpreting it.
- `check` groups distant `data-ref` warnings by the pair of places they link, with a count and up to three names. It also warns when a schematic or Mermaid diagram scrolls sideways in the pinned `.beside` column: lay it out tall or give it the full width.
- A Plot legend’s buttons are separate from the drawing’s image role so assistive technology can expose them as controls.
- `check` warns when text inside a `Chart` or `Plot` crosses the figure's edge (a label cut off on a phone) or overlaps other text (a legend over the axis labels, tick labels run together), at desktop and phone width, naming the figure and quoting the texts. A `Chart` is measured as `pack` drew it, a `Plot` as drawn and live.
- A `Chart`'s legend keeps its room under your own `grid: { bottom }` or `top`: the larger of your bottom and the legend's room is used, so the legend no longer covers the x-axis labels. A grid `height`, a legend position or grid bounds in a `media` option you set still win.
- A Chart using ECharts `baseOption` without a timeline no longer reports a missing timeline.
- Named stacked columns keep the category labels that would fit without series names. They can wrap a name beside a stack without taking room the category labels need.
- The guide asks for the decision after its evidence and on it (a `Question` with `rows`, `recommended` on yours), separates a reader's project context from their expertise (every name, part, revision and person identified where it first appears; a section after those it depends on), says which number a two-number finding's headline carries, keeps the accent's meaning the same in every figure, and keeps the contents rail for pages too long to scan. Schedules and owners stay in the tracker.
- `kit Chart` explains when an unpositioned legend reserves room, which settings keep your layout, and how to write responsive grid padding; `kit Explore` shows a frame returning a Chart; `kit Table` says `digits: 0` rounds; `kit Plot` says `digits: 0` rounds, that text labels keep the value axis's tick labels, and that crowded side-by-side values need height or fewer series.
- The skill's example asks which fix to try on the comparison's rows, with its recommendation marked, and keeps the wait on the curve rather than in a row of numbers beside it, with the accent on the second barista in every figure.

## v0.16.0

- A ```` ```diff-python ```` fence (`diff-ts`, `diff-<any language pack colours>`) colours each line's code in that language, the old and new side each read as one listing, and every diff fence tints its inserted and deleted lines. `pack` fails on a `diff-<language>` it does not colour; it used to stay plain without a word. A wrapped diff line continues under its code, not under its `+` or `-`.
- `Plot` marks take an arrow, `{ from: [x, y], to: [x, y], label }`, anchored to data: for a change the reader should follow, such as a peak moving. Labels keep off it and its label is placed as a note; it moves with the plot. In an `Explore`, `pack` warns about a short arrow, a crowded note or name, or unnamed lines in each frame it checks, naming the frame.
- Line names are never moved into a legend any more. A `Plot`'s named lines keep their names on the plot at every width: stacked at the ends with leaders, else beside their own line. A `Chart`'s crowded end names move apart about their ends. `pack` warns, naming the line and width, where a name finds no clear place: shorten it, add height, or end the lines further apart. A legend you set and the picked key stay.
- Stems and bars carry their names on the plot too, never in a legend. A stem's name sits beside its last stems; bars side by side name their series above one group (the last column group, the first row group), stacked in series order, each with a leader to its bar; a stack names its series inside or beside its segments. One named bar series gets no name: the plot's label says it. A horizontal bar plot grows a line per name. `legend: true` brings the legend back, and `pack` warns, naming the series and width, where a name finds no clear place.
- A `Chart`'s named lines keep their names at their ends beside your own grid bounds (`grid: { bottom: 24 }`) and an axis `min` or `max` that holds every point; these used to leave the lines with no names at all. Where the kit still cannot name them (zoom, a dataset, an explicit grid `width`, a reversed or second axis, a `textStyle`), `pack` says so: leave that out, or set a legend.

- `kit types` now uses the repository's strict browser options, including `exactOptionalPropertyTypes` and unchecked-index checks. Its output says it only prepares a config; `bun --check` performs semantic typechecking and reports the result. MDX expressions themselves are not typechecked.

Colours ECharts mixes from two of them, as a `visualMap` does, are drawn as those two mixed. Keep palette tokens opaque: `pack` reads a mix only between tokens the option lists together. A chart whose colours `pack` cannot write exactly (a colour function, a token mixed with a literal or translucent colour, `colorAlpha`, `colorLightness`, `colorSaturation`, `colorHue`, a visualMap's `opacity`, a `markArea` beside a translucent colour) is not drawn by `pack`.
- A chart's option functions (formatters) now also run in `pack`. A chart whose functions throw there, because they read the page, is not drawn by `pack` and appears when the script draws it, as before.
- A page rule that may match a figure's element and set its size, padding, border, `all`, `box-sizing` or an animation (or the root font size) leaves that figure to the script, as does a `height` that is not a length in px, rem, vw, vh, `clamp()`, `min()` or `max()`. A Plot's legend that wraps into a different number of rows across the widths a copy is shown at is not drawn either.
- For a layout that changes with the chart's width, write ECharts' `media` queries in the option rather than reading the element's width in a client component: the phone copy follows them, while code that measures the element runs only in the browser.
- A packed page grows by the drawings: about 10 to 20 KB per figure.
- One set of controls can drive several figures and readouts in different sections, with no client component: write `<Controls id="usage" controls={[...]} />` once (the same range, choice and toggle controls as `Explore`), then `<Explore use="usage" label="..." frame={daily} />` in any section after it. Each Explore takes `use` in place of `controls`, shows no row of its own, and keeps everything else (a Plot, a Chart, a readout, stats or a caption; frames `pack` computes or a `"use client"` module exports, `base`, `height`). `frame` gets all the set's values in the order the controls are written, so list the ones most figures read first and let a frame take fewer parameters. The choices are kept across reloads like any control with an id. A `Controls` and its Explores belong in one place: the page outside the Views, or one `View`, or one record of a filled `View` (a reader changes the controls of the place they look at). `pack` fails on a `use` with no `Controls` above it in its place (naming the sets that are), on `use` together with `controls`, on two `Controls` with one id, and on a frame that throws for a combination. Each Explore's precomputed frames still fit 100 KB, so add a control for what changes the figures. Each such figure also shows a quiet line of the chosen values ("7 days · Mac · Breakdown") that links back to the controls, kept in print, and left out on screen when the figure is written right under its own `Controls`; a toggle-only set with every toggle off says so ("Cached off", new label key `off`). `kit Explore` documents it (`kit Controls` prints the same).
- A written `Question` with a model answer inside is a self-check: what the reader writes is kept on the page, stays out of the reply and brings no Reply bar. One with nothing inside still joins the reply. A page that keeps Question answers without a Reply bar gets a Clear answers button at its end once one is saved, which asks before it clears the answers and leaves other controls as the reader set them (new label keys `clearAnswers`, `clearAnswersPrompt`). On a page with a Reply bar, a self-check's answer now brings the bar's Clear too.

## v0.10.0

One collection of records, shown as a grouped tree, a sortable table or a board, is one component: `Records`. It replaces `Tree`. Mermaid diagrams stay readable on phones and in print.

### What an author does differently

- Write `<Records {...plan} />` with a config object (`kit Records`): flat `data`, `idField`, `fields` with a kind each, `columns`, and `groupBy` for the tree's levels. `views`, `search`, `facets` and `sort` add the controls; the view, sort, search, filters and open folds go in the URL hash, so `<a href="#plan?f.owner=JV">` opens a filtered list. `link` names a filled `View` each row opens, with previous/next through the rows shown; `detail` names a text field each row opens to in place, in the tree, the table and the board alike.
- A field with `sum: true` adds subtotals and a total. Records without a value never count as zero: the sum shows what is known plus a question mark, `1,372 h + ?` (or `?` when no record has a value), since a missing value may be negative, and screen readers hear how many records it leaves out.
- A block inside a `View` keeps its state after the view's route: `#team?crew?view=table`. A view's route ends at its first `?`, and what follows belongs to what the view holds; client code reads and writes that part with `Views.local(el)` and `Views.setLocal(el, text)`, which keep the view's trail. Previous and next walk views by their route, so links that carry state (`#v/a?panel?x=1`) still get them, and follow each link with its state. `View`'s `param` takes the record id as it is; link to an id with `?`, `#` or `%` in it as `encodeURIComponent` writes it (`#s/a%3Fb`).
- `pack` checks the config and fails with the path of each problem (`fields.hours.kind`) and what to write instead. It also fails on a `link` that names no `View`, and on a block `id` that is also a View's name, in the page and in each record a filled `View` draws.
- `Tree` is gone. Flatten its nested `items` into records with the group's name as a field, list those fields in `groupBy`, and move a row's `text` and `ref` into fields named by `detail` and `refField`. Hand-written `.tree` markup still works. Uneven depths and JSX cells have no Records form; write the `<details>` yourself for those.
- New label keys for the block's controls and texts (`kit labels` lists them, all starting with `records`).

### Mermaid

- Mermaid diagrams stay readable on phones. A flowchart or state diagram too wide for a phone gets a second layout across its direction (left to right becomes top-down), shown below 40rem, so left to right is fine to write. Other diagrams shrink until their smallest text is 10 px, then scroll sideways. Printed, each diagram fits the paper whole, top-down when the left-to-right layout would be too small to read.
- A diagram's width limits are the custom properties `--mermaid-max` and `--mermaid-min` on its `svg`, so one page rule can change them.
- A circle node with a short label is about the size of a box node, not a large disc.
- Flowcharts turn back only the edges of a real cycle. An input written after the node it feeds now sits before that node, instead of being looped around it.
- `check`'s warning about tiny SVG text names the fix: drop the page rule that squeezes a diagram, or for a hand-built SVG, redraw it about 360 wide or hold it at the `min-width` it gives inside a `.scroll` block.

### check

- `check` waits after each load and `--do` step until the page has settled instead of a fixed time: fonts loaded, every visible chart drawn, and the timers of up to 1 s the page set have run. A slow island chart is in the shots, and an error a page throws shortly after a click is reported. What is still missing after 10 s is a problem: `still waiting after 10 s for charts to be drawn: div#sales.chart`.
- When Chrome fails to start or stops answering, `check` prints one line naming where it was (the screen, and the `--do` step if one was running) and exits 1 with no report, for example `error: the load event of file:///…/page.html did not come in 10 s (at dark narrow, view #fix/none)`. It used to wait forever.
- A `check` that stops with an error, or is ended by Ctrl-C or SIGTERM, leaves `--out` as it was: the PNGs of a run move there only once it has finished.
- `--tag` takes a name without a path separator; `--tag ../x` is a usage error, since it would write PNGs outside `--out`.
- `--timeout <seconds>` (default 600) limits the whole run, Chrome's start-up included: `error: check passed its 600 s limit at light desktop, view #plan/a, after 324 page(s): raise --timeout for a page with many views or --do steps`.
- Chrome exits with `check` however `check` ends, and its profile and temp files are deleted (also on Ctrl-C or SIGTERM, sent once or twice). Before, every run left a profile of up to 150 MB in the temp directory, and with Google Chrome a `com.google.Chrome.chrome_chrome_url_fetcher_.*` directory, and a `check` killed by a timeout left a Chrome running that used a CPU core.
- Chrome no longer downloads components or models while it checks a page.
- On Linux, a `TMPDIR` too deep for the socket Chrome keeps there (about 50 characters) stops `check` with `error: Chrome cannot start in a temp directory this deep (…): … set TMPDIR to a shorter one, such as /tmp`, instead of Chrome's stack trace.

## v0.9.0

Pages are MDX. An agent writes `page.mdx`: Markdown prose, the kit's components, and the `.ts` or `.json` data and `.css` it imports. `pack` renders it once to static HTML with Preact and ships code only for client components. Packed v0.8 files still open offline, and `check` still takes them.

### What an author does differently

- Write `page.mdx` instead of `page.html`; `pack` refuses any other input. There are no `kit:` references to write: `pack` adds the theme and the kit scripts the page uses.
- Prose, lists, tables and formulas are Markdown. A `#` and the paragraphs under it are the masthead; each `##` starts a section; a Markdown table is a `table.compare`.
- Figures and interactions are components imported from `"kit"`. Behaviour no component covers goes in your own `.tsx` component whose first line is `"use client"`.
- Page settings move into frontmatter: `lang` instead of `<html lang>`, `labels` instead of `var kitLabels`, `scheme` instead of `<html data-scheme>`.
- Controls with ids are kept across reloads without `reply.js`. Ids repeated on one page fail `pack`.
- Images and fonts that code shows are imported (`import icon from "./icon.svg"` gives a `data:` URL `pack` embeds). A file path written in client code, its props or a filled view's records fails `pack`, since the browser would load it later.
- `example.html` is now `example/` (`page.mdx` and the model it imports).

Before (v0.8.2), a step-through figure:

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="kit:theme.css">
<figure class="fig" data-stepper data-start="1">
  <svg viewBox="0 0 90 20"><rect data-step="1" width="20" height="20" /><rect data-step="2" x="35" width="20" height="20" /></svg>
  <ol data-captions><li>The queue is empty</li><li>A burst arrives</li></ol>
</figure>
<script src="kit:steps.js"></script>
```

After (v0.9.0):

```mdx
import { Stepper } from "kit";

<Stepper start={1} captions={["The queue is empty", "A burst arrives"]}>
  <svg viewBox="0 0 90 20"><rect data-step="1" width="20" height="20" /><rect data-step="2" x="35" width="20" height="20" /></svg>
</Stepper>
```

### New public names

Components, imported from `"kit"` (`kit <Name>` documents each):

- `Chart`: `option`, `label`, `class`, `setup`. Formatters and labels may be JSON (`{ digits: 1, unit: "min" }`, `{ percent: true }`, `{ currency: "EUR", compact: true }`, or one text per item).
- `Plot`: `option` (`series`, `categories`, `horizontal`, `stack`, `x`, `y`, `legend`), `label`, `class`. Axis `format` and series `label` take the same JSON formats; numbers follow the page's `lang`.
- `Explore`: `controls` (each `{ id, label }` with `range: [min, max, step]`, `choice` or `toggle: true`, and `value`, `format`), `frame`, `base`, `label`, `class`. A slider, switch or toggles changing a plot or chart, readout, `stats` and caption; `frame` runs in the browser when a `"use client"` module exports it, else `pack` computes every frame.
- `Stepper`: `captions`, `captionsFirst`, `start`, `label`, `class`, `onStep`, `restartOn` (back to step 1 when it changes).
- `View`: `name`, `param`, `back` for a static view per record; `records` and `render` fill one view from data.
- `Tree`: `items`, `row`, `cols`, `head`; types `TreeItem`, `TreeRow`.
- `Table`: `rows`, `cols` (each `head`, `value`, `format`, `link`, `note`), `state`.
- `Question`: `name`, `legend`, `options`, `multiple`, `checked`, `answer` (a self-check that marks the choice and opens the explanation written inside). Without `options` it takes a written answer.

Frontmatter keys: `title`, `lang`, `labels`, `reply: true`, `scheme: light | dark`.

Math: `$...$` in a sentence and `$$` on lines of their own render to MathML when the page packs (`kit math.css`, with the formula sheet pattern).

Slots: Markdown between a client component's tags is its `children`, rendered once and placed with `{children}`.

CLI: `pack page.mdx -o out.html`; `kit` lists components and features, `kit <Name>` documents one, `kit page` the page conventions, `kit labels` prints every kit text as a frontmatter `labels` block to translate, `kit types > tsconfig.json` then `bun check` typechecks the page's `.ts` and `.tsx` with nothing to install.

### Removed

- HTML as `pack` input, and the `kit:` file references in a page.
- `kit <file>` for the kit scripts that components now write for you (`views.js`, `steps.js`, `charts.js`, `plot.js`); `kit link`, `kit reply` and `kit keep` document the attributes that stay.
- `var kitLabels` in a page script; use the frontmatter `labels`.
