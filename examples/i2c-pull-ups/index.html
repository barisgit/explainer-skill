The 10 kΩ pull-ups take 847 ns to raise the line. At 400 kHz the bus allows 300.

A 3.3 V bus with about 100 pF on each line, timed from 0.3 to 0.7 VDD as the I²C specification times it.

    Nothing drives the line high but a resistor

    The MCU and the sensor can only pull SDA and SCL low. When they let go, RSDA has to charge the line's capacitance CSDA on its own, and the time that takes grows with the product of the two.

    At 100 kHz an 847 ns edge fits inside the 1000 ns Standard-mode allows. At 400 kHz the edge outlasts the 300 ns the specification allows, so SDA may still be under 0.7 VDD, the lowest level UM10204 counts as high (Table 10), when the sensor reads it.

    Any standard value from 1 kΩ to 3.3 kΩ fits; 2.2 kΩ leaves room

    3.3 kΩ only just fits, at 280 ns, if the 100 pF estimate is right. 2.2 kΩ still fits up to 161 pF.

    Where 0.8473 comes from

    Released at t=0, the line charges towards VDD as V(t)=VDD(1−e−t/RC). It crosses 0.3 VDD at t1=RCln⁡10.7 and 0.7 VDD at t2=RCln⁡10.3, so

    tr=t2−t1=RCln⁡0.70.3=0.8473RCb=0.8473⋅10kΩ⋅100pF=847ns

    The bounds follow: Rmax=tr/(0.8473Cb), which is 3.54 kΩ at 400 kHz, and Rmin=(VDD−0.4V)/IOL, which is 967 Ω assuming only the minimum guaranteed sink of 3 mA at 0.4 V.
