    The sines shrink as 1/n, and only the odd ones are there

      Only odd harmonics appear, with bn=4/(nπ). Cutting the sum at k terms throws away a tail that is still large near the corner.

      The peak narrows as 1/k but its height tends to 1.179

      Differentiate the k-term sum: the cosines add up in closed form.

      Sk′(x)=4π∑j=1kcos⁡((2j−1)x)=2πsin⁡2kxsin⁡x

      The first zero, the peak, is at x=π/2k. Put t=2kx and use sin⁡x≈x for small x:

      Sk(π2k)→2π∫0πsin⁡ttdt=2πSi(π)

      That is 1.179. More terms move the peak towards the jump and its height towards this nonzero limit.

        The overshoot does not vanish. In what sense does the series still converge to the square wave?
        Show answer

        At every fixed point away from the jump the sum converges, since the peak moves past it. At the jump itself it gives the midpoint, 0. The convergence is not uniform: arbitrarily close to the jump, any finite sum is near 0 while the wave is ±1, an error approaching half the jump. Separately, the excess above the plateau tends to 8.9% of the jump.
