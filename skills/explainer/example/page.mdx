## Why one clump of customers costs an hour

<figure class="fig" id="mechanism" aria-label="How a backlog grows">

```mermaid
flowchart TD
	clump["4 customers in one minute"] --> backlog["Backlog: 6 minutes of work"]
	backlog --> spare{"Spare time?"}
	spare -->|"50% busy"| calm["Clear in 12 min"]
	spare -->|"90% busy"| slow["Clear in 60 min"]
	slow --> stack["Next clump stacks on top"]
	stack --> backlog
```

</figure>

{/* data-ref terms light the diagram part with the same Mermaid id (kit link.js). */}

1. <span data-ref="clump">Four customers</span> walk in within the same minute. Random arrivals do this several times a day.
2. One is served, three wait: a <span data-ref="backlog">backlog</span> of 6 minutes on top of everyone who arrives next.
3. The backlog shrinks only in <span data-ref="spare">spare time</span>, the minutes when nobody new is ordering.
4. At 50% busy every other minute is spare: <span data-ref="calm">the 6 minutes are gone in about 12</span>.
5. At 90% busy one minute in ten is spare: <span data-ref="slow">the same backlog takes about an hour</span>.
6. Within that hour <span data-ref="stack">the next clump</span> arrives and stacks on top. That is where the 18-minute average comes from.
