# What made pages better or worse

Each point comes from comparing generated pages side by side. Use judgment where your subject differs.

- **First screen:** the answer in one sentence and the figure that shows it. Pages that put every filter, summary number, and caveat up front read as a wall; controls for later questions belong in those sections.
- **Interaction that reveals:** each control answers a question the reader would ask and changes something visible at once. The ones readers valued: hiding the dominant part so the rest becomes readable, playing through several scenarios rather than one path, presets that can honestly end in "nothing fits", clicking a mark or row to open its own view.
- **Explanations:** offer several playable cases. Each step shows where we are, what just changed, and what has accumulated so far. One narrow walkthrough lost to broader pages every time.
- **Stepping:** step through something when its order is the point: an algorithm's rounds, a sequence of messages, a derivation. A total built from parts, such as a cost waterfall or a breakdown, reads at a glance: show it finished with the values on the marks. Back, Play and Next around it cost a row and reveal nothing. Steppers work on HTML and hand-built SVG; a Mermaid diagram shows whole.
- **Study material:** a page that took a course's slides and added every widget it could lost the point. Start from the questions the reader must answer and the source's own figures; give each idea at most one interaction, where moving it builds intuition; put answers behind a click so the reader tries first; end with a formula sheet. Include `kit:reply.js` so the reader's inputs and notes survive a reload; give inputs ids. Fidelity to the source beats extra scope.
- **Words:** each line says something the page does not already say. Skip the kicker above the title or a section heading, subtitles that restate a heading, hints that reword their label, advice on how to read a number, and notes about the page itself, such as where it saves; the headline number appears once on the first screen. A short cue for an interaction the reader could not guess, such as clicking a bar to drill down, earns its place. Readers took the rest as filler.
- **Live text:** a headline or count that depends on controls updates with them, or is phrased as a claim about the default view. Labels and explanations never contradict the current state.
- **Surfaces:** a box tells the reader that its controls act on what is inside it. The kit boxes a figure that holds inputs, tabs or a stepper and leaves a static chart, diagram or table plain on the page, in line with the text; add `.panel` only when a static figure must read as one object. Pages where every block sat in the same card read as monotone.
- **Rhythm:** give a section the weight of its question rather than the shape of the one before. Set supporting points beside their figure (`.beside`), method, sources and assumptions as `.minor`, and the one place where the reader decides or plays as a `.band`. Colour the mark that answers the section with the accent and keep the rest neutral. A page whose sections all share one shape reads as a template.
- **Charts:** use the zoom, legend toggles, animated switching between views, and click-to-drill that ECharts already has. Give exact values without hover, through a readout or a table behind a disclosure.
- **Diagrams:** Mermaid for structure, flows, sequences, and states; top-down layouts stay readable on phones. For a process whose order matters, show the diagram whole beside its steps as a list whose key terms light their part (`data-ref`). Keep node labels to a few words and let the text carry the sentence; longer labels wrap at about 30 characters into tall boxes. Hand-build SVG when a diagram must step or respond in ways highlighting cannot. An SVG file in `<img>` follows the theme through `currentColor` and the kit variables (`var(--ink)`, `var(--accent)`); write the SVG inline when its parts must be linked or interactive.
- **Asking the reader:** when the page ends in a choice only the reader can make, ask it on the page as a reply question rather than in chat text.
- **Phones:** stack and reflow. Nothing scrolls sideways except data tables.
- **Honesty:** unknown is not no; mark inferred claims as inferred; a claim never exceeds its source, especially about security.
- **Colour:** use the theme tokens so light and dark both work.
