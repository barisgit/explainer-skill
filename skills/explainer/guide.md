# What made pages better or worse

Each point comes from comparing generated pages side by side. Use judgment where your subject differs.

## The reader

- **Questions first:** infer the reader's knowledge, goal and available time from the request; ask when unknown and the answer would change the page. Organise around their questions, not the data's hierarchy. Aim for about one screen per reader question, with the rest in a folded reference band; completeness is not the main path. Give experienced readers a quick route beside novice orientation. For a lost or time-pressed reader, offer one task at a time and a small progress map, not a whole course. A section's heading states its answer and its body shows it; folds and views hold supporting detail, not the finding. Short `data-nav` labels keep the contents scannable.
- **First screen:** use one short headline, not several decisions joined together; the lede says what it rests on, not a list of negatives. Name the next action and its size directly beneath the answer, and put that action's section first, reference after. Briefly orient novices. The first figure carries the deciding relation, not a flowchart restating the prose. Keep later filters and caveats with their questions.

## Many records

- **Story in front, records behind:** keep many records (tasks, costs, findings, requirements) in one data collection and compute every number in the story from it. Story sections give the essence, each claim a link that opens the branch of the complete list it rests on (illustrative: "Spike boards: 12 tasks, 70 h →"). The list stays on the main page, every item in the source's own order (its numbering, sequence or chronology), grouped with subtotals; views add depth: a static `View` per record for a few, or one filled from `records` for a dozen or more, or heavy ones; print shows the list and static views, a filled view only while open. Without the list readers hopped between views to see the plan, and a plan sorted by hours lost the lead, who knew its tasks by their work-list numbers. Sort by value only where the ranking is the answer, and call it a ranking. A view tells one thing's own story, not the page's table filtered to rows that are mostly zero.
- **Hierarchy as disclosure:** a collection goes in the kit's `Records`, grouped by its own fields, each row a summary line with its numbers; its table and board show the same records sorted or by status. A filter highlights matches in the tree rather than flattening it into a filtered table. Each item that opens says what it is, why it matters, and what done means where the source says so; each group gets a sentence on what it delivers. A row carries only what readers compare across rows; the rest goes into the opened item, without repeating the row. A marker (a `.chip` beside its word) encodes a category readers scan across rows, never decoration. A ninety-item plan shown as a flat table of hours and prices, never saying what each item was, left its lead unable to defend the price.

## Judging every item

- **Each item whole, where it is judged:** when the reader must approve, decide or answer for every item, a decisions review or an approval list, show each one complete with its question beside it: the situation (what the reader does and what happens), what happens now, the alternative (and what comparable tools do, when known) and a recommendation. A review of fifty decisions built as a grouped list with a drill-down per item, groups collapsed, sent its owner, back after two days, into every item for context and gave no way to answer in the list. For single-choice reviews, use `Records` with `open`, `reply` and `width: "full"`, so items read and are answered in their rows; otherwise keep each complete item and its `Question` together, not behind a click or collapsed group.
- **Only what needs the reader:** list items needing a call; fold the rest. Show answered/total, keeping headline and form counts consistent. Label options A/B/C beside their evidence and mark the recommendation on the choice itself. Include "both", "test first" or "need more context" when real outcomes, and use `Question multiple` when several may be chosen (`Records.reply` is single-choice); a forced single choice can misrepresent the decision. Define unfamiliar terms where first used.
- **Ask when the source cannot say:** when the source gives an item less than the reader needs to judge it, ask whoever asked for the page before building. A value the source does not give stays empty, never inferred from its group's heading: a page that marked every item in a group "held" for that reason was wrong for each of them.

## Explaining

- **Interaction that reveals:** a control answers a reader's question and changes something visible at once. Start a model at its smallest instructive size. Beside the first control, say what to try and what to notice (illustrative: "Change the input; notice which branch lights up"), and offer a way back to a meaningful default. Prefer aligned small multiples when comparing states, rather than making readers remember a switched-away view. Dependent headlines and counts update with controls or name the default state; labels and explanations stay true to the current state. Content no control changes stays outside client components, so it shows before scripts run and prints. Ask reader-only decisions on the page as reply questions, not in chat.
- **Markdown first:** prose, lists, tables and formulas stay Markdown, also between a client component's tags (its `{children}`). In JSX inside an expression such as `items.map(...)`, backticks are plain text and a `{x}` between them is evaluated. Your own component updates a control's state with a functional `setState((s) => ...)`, or values restored on reload are lost. Typecheck: `kit types > tsconfig.json`, then `bun check`.
- **Stepping:** step through something only when its order is the point. Offer several playable cases, each step showing where we are, what just changed and what has accumulated; one narrow walkthrough lost to broader pages every time. A total built from parts, such as a cost waterfall, reads at a glance: show it finished with the values on the marks, since Back, Play and Next around it cost a row and reveal nothing.
- **Study material:** start from the source's figures, not every widget available. For unfamiliar material, show a worked example before practice; "try first" suits revision, not missing basics. Usually one interaction per idea is enough; add another only when it teaches something distinct. Keep a formula sheet as reference (`kit math.css`). Practice uses plausible choices (`Question` with `answer`) or a written answer (`Question` without options, the model answer inside, outside any reply), hiding the explanation and figures that give it away. Practice headings ask rather than reveal the answer.

## Words and trust

- **Words:** cut repetition that adds no understanding, not useful summaries, labels or worked examples. Leave page-making metadata out: source keys, tracker/date lines, reply-button instructions and "how this was made" sections are not subject evidence. Skip kickers, restated headings and reworded labels; keep cues that help the reader act. Use supplied figures as inputs, with a control when the result depends on them; earlier work appears as data, named with its sources.
- **Basis and honesty:** link claims to sources beside the evidence, especially actionable values; put each candidate's datasheet/shop links in a consistent place on it, not buried in prose. Keep a number's method, assumptions and comparison nearby, with detail in reference. State an item's uncertainty once as a status, not in every cell. Unknown is not no; claims stay no stronger than sources. Mark the page's own reasoning beside its claim; omit assumptions that would not change the reader's action.

## Showing

- **Show the subject:** where the subject has a structure or appearance, show it: a block diagram, the system in its context, the flow of the process being changed, or a photo, render or mockup when one exists. Charts of its numbers are not a picture of it. Import real photos, drawings or datasheet crops of the thing being judged as local files, readable in size and captioned with what each proves; catalogue thumbnails that prove nothing do not count. Offline means no network is needed, not no links: keep source links, and use a linked still for media that cannot work offline. No `file://` links in pages others receive; they point into one person's machine. Show a process whose order matters whole, beside the list of its steps, its labels a few words while the text carries the sentence. A circuit is a ```` ```schematic ```` block: a netlist whose parts you place relative to each other, so the wiring you state is the wiring drawn (`kit schematic`); label parts by their names, link them to the prose with `data-ref`, and keep it to what the section explains, a stage or a loop rather than the whole board. Hand-build inline SVG for a picture Mermaid and a schematic cannot draw, or for parts that step or respond beyond highlighting: a `viewBox` about 360 wide with 14-unit text reads on a phone, its displayed width capped for wide screens; strokes and text in `currentColor` (`var(--ink)`), other colours in CSS from the kit variables, `var(--accent)` for the part in question; each part a `<g data-ref>` that its name in the prose shares. An SVG file in `<img>` follows the theme the same way, but its parts cannot be linked, and one a client component draws keeps the file's own colours.
- **Charts:** add zoom, legend toggles, animated switching or click-to-drill only when they help answer the question, not as defaults. Give exact values without hover, through a readout or disclosed table. A single value is a number (`.stats`), not a one-bar chart. Colour what the reader compares consistently across charts and views; greying every mark but one looked washed out.
- **Layout:** give a section the weight of its question rather than the shape of the one before; a page whose sections all share one shape reads as a template. A box tells the reader that its controls act on what is inside it, so add `.panel` only where a static figure must read as one object; pages where every block sat in the same card read as monotone. On phones, stack and reflow: nothing scrolls sideways except data tables, wide formulas and diagrams too wide to shrink to readable text.
- **Language:** a page not in English sets `lang` in its frontmatter, and `labels` translated from `kit labels`; English controls on a Slovenian page read as unfinished.
